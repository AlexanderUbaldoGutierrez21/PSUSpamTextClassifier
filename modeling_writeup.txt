Modeling Efforts for Spam Text Classification

Challenges in the data:
- The dataset is imbalanced, with only about 13.5% spam messages (538 out of 4000), which can lead to models biased towards the majority class.
- Texts contain special characters, URLs, numbers, and abbreviations, requiring careful preprocessing.
- Short message lengths make feature extraction challenging, as spam and ham may share common words.

Data preprocessing:
- Converted text to lowercase.
- Removed punctuation and numbers to focus on words.
- Used TF-IDF vectorization with English stop words and limited to 5000 features to reduce dimensionality.

Models tried and representation:
- Multinomial Naive Bayes: Simple probabilistic model, represented data as TF-IDF vectors.
- Logistic Regression: Linear model for binary classification.
- Linear SVM: Support vector machine with linear kernel for better performance on text.

Steps to avoid overfitting:
- Used 5-fold stratified cross-validation to evaluate models on unseen data.
- Selected a simple linear model (LinearSVC) which performed best without complex hyperparameters.
- Limited vectorizer features to prevent overfitting to noise.

What could be improved:
- Handle class imbalance with techniques like SMOTE or class weighting.
- Experiment with n-grams or stemming/lemmatization for better features.
- Try ensemble methods or neural networks, but constrained by allowed libraries.
- With more time, explore hyperparameter tuning or feature engineering like URL detection.

Do I trust the model?
- Reasonably, as it achieved high balanced accuracy (~0.92) in cross-validation. However, the imbalance means it might still miss some spam, and real-world performance depends on test data similarity.
